Authors: Gaia Grossi, Leonardo Vanni. Written 2026-10-18.
Targets, in order: Zenodo DOI for the code, arXiv q-bio.PE preprint, submission to
Letters in Biomathematics (LiB). Backup venue: Spora (needs a faculty mentor as

## 0. Framing (decided)

The paper is a reproducible case study, not a new model. Title direction:
"Stochastic establishment, immune activation failure and the emergence of drug
resistance in a two-strain within-host viral model: exact simulation versus
deterministic and branching-process predictions". Final title picked at the end.

Three quantitative claims, each with simulation and an analytical check:

1. Establishment from a small inoculum follows branching-process theory; the
   deterministic model overestimates establishment.
2. When the immune response starts from a few cells, stochastic failure of immune
   activation turns a deterministic acute (cleared) infection into a chronic one
   with a computable probability. This replaces the mis-labelled "4.9 percent
   establishment" result of the course notebook.
3. Under therapy, deterministic competitive release predicts certain takeover of
   the resistant mutant; stochastically it happens with a probability that depends
   on treatment start and efficacy, splits into pre-existing versus de novo origin,
   and is predicted well by the branching extinction probability of the mutant
   lineages present at treatment start.

Plus one global figure: the stochastic regime map on the beta x k grid.

Honesty constraints to keep in the text: parameter values are illustrative and not
fitted to data; single mutant, no back mutation; no latent reservoir; no
pharmacokinetics; mass-action CTL dynamics.

## 3. Science work packages

### WP1 Fast exact SSA (blocks everything else). ~1 day.
- Port the existing direct method to Numba. Precompute propensity update, avoid
  per-event allocation, record on a fixed grid (for example 0.05 day) instead of
  every event. Return final state, extinction time of each lineage (WT, mutant,
  CTL), and the maximum of each species.
- Validate: (i) all rates zero keeps state constant, (ii) pure birth-death subsystem
  matches its exact mean and variance, (iii) mean of 1000 runs in the chronic regime
  tracks the ODE, (iv) distribution of WT extinction times from the Numba SSA matches
  the original pure-Python SSA on 200 runs (two-sample KS test, p > 0.01).
- Budget check, measured 2026-10-18 (`make bench`): about 3e7 events/s compiled.
  The chronic set runs 12 million events per 120-day realisation in 0.39 s; the
  other presets take milliseconds. No lambda rescaling and no tau-leaping needed.
- Status 2026-10-18: done. `viraldyn/ssa.py`, validated by `tests/test_ssa.py`
  (items i to iv above, plus seeding, grid recording and early-stop semantics).

### WP2 Establishment versus branching theory. ~0.5 day theory, 0.5 day compute.
- Theory: a free virion infects with probability pi = beta T* / (c + beta T*),
  else is cleared. An infected cell produces virions at rate p and dies at rate
  delta + k E0, so its offspring count is geometric. Extinction probability from
  one virion q_V solves q_V = (1 - pi) + pi q_I, q_I = d / (d + p (1 - q_V)), with
  d = delta + k E0. P_est(V0) = 1 - q_V^V0. Same formulas for the mutant with its
  own beta, p. Check against Pearson, Krapivsky and Perelson 2011.
- Definition of establishment: cumulative infected cells reach 100 before total
  virus plus infected cells hits zero. Threshold crossing, not "state at day 30".
- Experiments: P_est versus V0 in {1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20}, immune off (k = 0) and
  immune at fixed E0 with q = 0, N = 2000 per point. One figure: theory curves,
  SSA points with Wilson intervals, inset with example trajectories.
- Also report the R0 of the course notebook's establishment parameter set (33) to
  make the point that the old result could not have been bottleneck extinction.
- Status 2026-10-18: done. `viraldyn/theory.py` (two-type generating-function
  fixed point, closed form pi (1 - 1/R0) for one virion), `scripts/exp_establishment.py`
  (68 conditions x 2000 runs, 2 s on 16 cores), `scripts/fig4_establishment.py`.
  Theory falls inside the 95 percent interval in 64 of 68 conditions, pooled z = -0.17,
  largest absolute deviation 0.034. Panel (b) adds the R0 sweep for V0 in {1, 5}
  against the deterministic step.

### WP3 Immune activation failure. ~1 day theory, 0.5 day compute.
- Phenomenon: with E0 small, q I(t) E versus d_E E is a time-inhomogeneous linear
  birth-death process for the CTL lineage, forced by the infected-cell load I(t).
  If every CTL lineage dies before expanding, the deterministic acute clearance does
  not happen and the infection becomes chronic.
- Theory: Kendall's extinction probability for a linear birth-death process with
  birth rate q I(t) and death rate d_E, using I(t) from the ODE as a first
  approximation. Derive carefully; state that I(t) is itself random and the
  approximation is tested, not assumed.
- Experiments: P(activation failure) versus E0 in {1, 2, 3, 5, 10} and versus q,
  N = 2000 per point, conditional on viral establishment. Figure with theory curve
  and SSA points. Second panel: the old notebook figure redrawn, with trajectories
  coloured by CTL lineage fate rather than by "extinct versus established".
- Status 2026-10-18: done, with two changes found by the data. (1) q is a weak
  lever (failure 7 to 3 percent across two decades of q) because the infected-cell
  load crosses d_E/q within hours at R0 = 33; the figure sweeps the effector death
  rate d_E instead (failure 0.6 to 91 percent over d_E in [0.03, 3]), and the q sweep
  stays in the CSV for one sentence. (2) A variant with k = 0 isolates the lineage
  process; given the infected-cell path it is exactly a linear birth-death process,
  and Kendall's formula evaluated on each realisation's own path matches it. The
  deterministic-forcing approximation runs about 3 percent (relative) low because
  conditioned-on-survival loads grow faster than the ODE. With E0 = 1 and the preset,
  P(failure | established) = 0.051 [0.041, 0.063], theory 0.045: this is the course
  notebook's "4.9 percent established". Above d_E of about 1 per day the full model
  exceeds the theory through a second route, the expanded response collapsing before
  clearance; the CSV splits the two routes by the peak effector count (E_EXPANDED = 10).
  Files: `viraldyn/theory.py` (kendall_extinction, immune_failure_probability),
  `scripts/exp_immune_activation.py`, `scripts/fig5_immune_activation.py`,
  `tests/test_immune_activation.py`. The SSA gained a per-species cap (`stop_above`)
  because nothing limits the effectors when k = 0.

### WP4 Competitive release and resistance emergence. ~0.5 day setup, compute
overnight, 1 day analysis.
- Deterministic: keep the existing two-phase ODE figure; add the derivation that
  takeover requires R0_wt (1 - eps) < 1 < R0_mut.
- Stochastic ensemble figure: N = 1000, show median and 5 to 95 percent band, not
  the mean, since the mean of zeros and oscillations misleads.
- Main experiment: grid of treatment start t_treat in {5, 10, 20, 30, 50} days and
  efficacy eps in {0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.99}, N = 1000 per cell. Outcome: mutant
  infected cells plus mutant virus above zero at t_treat + 120 days and mutant
  virus above 100 at that time. Record whether mutant lineage present at t_treat
  (pre-existing) or arose after (de novo) by tracking the first mutation event
  time after t_treat and the mutant state at t_treat.
- Theory check: P_emerge ~ 1 - q_Vm^{Vm(t_treat)} q_Im^{Im(t_treat)} with the
  mutant branching probabilities evaluated at the post-treatment target-cell level,
  computed per realisation from the recorded state at t_treat and then averaged.
  Plot predicted versus observed per grid cell.
- Also report time from treatment start to mutant dominance (mutant virus exceeds
  WT virus) with its distribution.
- Compute budget: 20 cells x 1000 runs at 0.4 s per run is about 2.2 core-hours,
  about 10 minutes on 16 cores. N can go to 5000 if the intervals need it.
- Note from the benchmark: with the notebook's "therapy" preset a virion infects
  with probability 0.048, so from 10 virions most stochastic runs go extinct before
  establishing. Choose the pre-treatment inoculum (or use the chronic preset, as the
  notebook's stochastic therapy section did) so that establishment is near certain,
  and say why in Methods.
- Status 2026-10-18: done, with design changes forced by the data.
  (1) The notebook's therapy preset is not stochastically viable: its deterministic
  chronic infection passes through 3e-8 virions at the first trough (day 17.5) and
  none of 1000 realisations survives to day 100, even from the deterministic set
  point (2 infected cells). The deterministic competitive-release figure of the
  notebook therefore rests on a trajectory that would have ended in clearance.
  Recorded in results/preset_viability.csv. (2) New "resistance" preset: same R0
  (4.8 wild type, 3.9 mutant, suppression threshold eps* = 0.79), slower and weaker
  effector response (q = 0.002, k = 0.003) so that the set point is I* = d_E/q = 50
  infected cells and about 1200 virions; all 1000 realisations survive 100 days and a
  mutant lineage is present at day 30 in about 15 percent of them. Start state: 10
  infected cells, 10 effectors. (3) Grid t_treat in {5,10,20,30,50} x eps in
  {0.5,0.7,0.8,0.9,0.99}, N = 1000, horizon 120 days, emergence = 100 mutant infection
  events during therapy. The deterministic model predicts mutant takeover in all 25
  cells. Stochastically: below 2 percent whenever the drug suppresses the wild type
  (eps >= 0.8; cure in 98 to 100 percent of runs), 23 to 29 percent at eps = 0.5
  where the wild type persists and keeps producing mutants that are fitter under
  drug (slow replacement, median 60 days), 5 to 8 percent at eps = 0.7. (4) Theory
  per realisation uses the wild-type-only deterministic path as the environment
  (the first version used the path with the mutant in it and underpredicted five
  times). It matches at eps >= 0.8 and eps = 0.5 and fails at eps = 0.7 (predicts 42
  percent) because there the drug pushes the wild type itself through a trough it
  survives in only 6 percent of realisations, so the de novo supply the theory
  assumes never materialises. Say this plainly in the paper. (5) System-size sweep at
  (30, 0.9) scaling lam, 1/beta, 1/q, 1/k and the state by Omega in {1,3,10,30,100}:
  P(emergence) 0.8, 1.4, 6.8, 21, 52 percent, inside the theory band at every size;
  cure still 48 percent at Omega = 100. Files: scripts/exp_resistance.py (10 min on
  16 cores, dominated by the Omega = 100 runs), scripts/fig6_competitive_release.py,
  scripts/fig7_resistance.py, tests/test_resistance.py, results/resistance*.csv,
  results/competitive_release_ensemble.npz, results/preset_viability.csv.

## 4. Tests (pytest, run in CI)

- Stoichiometry: every column of S conserves what it should (infection: dT + dI = 0,
  dV = -1; production and clearance change one species by one).
- Propensities are non-negative for any non-negative state and parameters.
- R0 formula equals the spectral radius of the next-generation matrix of the
  linearised infection subsystem, for three parameter sets.
- SSA constant under zero rates; SSA mean versus ODE in the large-population regime
  within 3 standard errors on a coarse grid.
- Branching fixed point versus SSA extinction frequency, immune off, N = 2000,
  within the Wilson interval.
- Every figure script runs from the committed CSVs with no network and no SSA call
  (`make figures` under 2 minutes).
